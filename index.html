Bulletproof Your Brand: A Simple Guide to Business Insurance

Running a business involves more than growing sales and serving customers. Unexpected accidents, legal claims and other risks can create significant financial problems. Business insurance can help protect a company against some of these risks.

The right policy depends on the type of business, its employees, property, customers and the risks associated with its activities.

Why Business Insurance Matters

Business insurance can provide financial protection when unexpected events affect your company. It can also help businesses satisfy contractual or legal requirements.

  • Financial protection: Helps manage certain covered claims and legal expenses.
  • Compliance: Some businesses may need specific coverage depending on local requirements.
  • Business continuity: Certain policies can help reduce the financial impact of an interruption.
  • Contract requirements: Some clients, landlords or vendors may require proof of insurance.
  • Peace of mind: Appropriate coverage can make it easier to concentrate on running the business.

Common Business Insurance Policies

There is no single insurance package that works for every company. These are some of the common types of business coverage.

General Liability

Designed to help protect businesses from certain third-party injury, property damage and related claims.

Typical cost varies by business

Professional Liability

Often relevant to consultants and service businesses. It can address certain claims involving professional errors or negligence.

Cost depends on risk

Workers' Compensation

Coverage that may apply to workplace injuries and employee benefits, depending on applicable laws.

Depends on payroll and risk

Ways to Manage Insurance Costs

  1. Compare coverage: Review quotes and policy terms from multiple insurers.
  2. Match coverage to your risks: Avoid paying for coverage that does not fit your actual business activities.
  3. Improve workplace safety: Good safety practices may reduce business risk.
  4. Review your policy regularly: Business operations change, so insurance needs can change too.

Quick FAQ

How much coverage does a business need?

The appropriate amount depends on factors such as the industry, revenue, employees, contracts and potential risks.

What is the difference between general and professional liability?

General liability generally focuses on certain third-party bodily injury and property damage claims, while professional liability relates to certain professional services and errors.

Can insurance premiums be tax deductible?

Business insurance expenses may be deductible in some circumstances. Tax treatment depends on the business and applicable tax rules.
